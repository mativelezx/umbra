# Founder Communication Contract

> Portable contract for founder-led communication, personal narrative, social distribution, build-in-public, launch storytelling, audience trust, and the founder data package.

Use this during project bootstrap, GTM planning, founder-led sales, launch/waitlist work, social/content strategy, public narrative decisions, brand voice decisions, investor/CTO handoff, crisis communication, and any feature whose adoption depends on the founder's public trust.

## Principle

Founder communication is product distribution and trust infrastructure.

For a solo founder, public communication is not "marketing after the product." It is how the market learns the problem, how early users qualify themselves, how feedback comes back, and how trust forms before the product is fully proven.

Core rule:

> Do not publish, launch, price, reposition, or refactor around a public promise until the founder narrative, audience, channel, proof, CTA, product state, and business decision agree.


Rules:

- Personal data is not automatically public just because it exists in the repo.
- The founder story should explain earned insight, not perform vulnerability for engagement.
- Every personal anecdote needs a reason: credibility, empathy, lesson, product thesis, or user connection.
- A founder can be authentic without being unfiltered.
- If the product handles AI, data, money, clients, or creative identity, founder communication must over-index on trust and clarity.
- Raw founder packages, CVs, transcripts, social exports, DMs, private notes, and model-inferred founder biographies must be converted into an intake artifact before becoming product, GTM, pitch, landing, social, or support truth.
- The intake artifact must separate public-safe facts, private/context-only facts, inferred claims, contradictions, proof needing permission, and founder approvals needed.

## Authenticity Rules

Authentic founder communication is specific, evidenced, and reversible.

Use:

- concrete moments;
- decisions in progress;
- lessons from real work;
- product screenshots or metrics when safe;
- mistakes with fixes;
- founder taste and tradeoffs;
- user problems heard directly;
- honest uncertainty with next step.

Avoid:

- generic AI-written lessons;
- fake vulnerability;
- pretending every post is spontaneous;
- engagement bait;
- guru voice;
- exaggerated progress;
- "we" when it is really "I", or "I" when users/team deserve credit;
- copying another founder's cadence, controversy, phrasing, screenshots, or persona.

## Founder Channel And Company Channel

Some projects need both a founder/personal channel and a company/product channel.

These channels should be related, but not interchangeable.

```md
Founder/personal channel:

Founder channel audience:
Founder channel voice:
Founder channel CTA:
Founder channel proof:
Company/product channel:
Company channel role:
Company channel audience:
Company channel voice:
Company channel CTA:
Company channel proof:
Shared content:
Content that belongs only to founder:
Content that belongs only to company:
Cross-posting rules:
Reply/DM ownership:
Escalation/support path:
Metrics by channel:
```

Rules:

- The founder channel usually builds human trust, taste, story, decisions, public learning, and founder-led distribution.
- The company/product channel usually builds product clarity, onboarding, releases, proof, support, community updates, demos, trust/legal information, and repeatable brand memory.
- The founder can explain why the product exists; the company channel must explain what the product does and how to use it.
- Do not make the company account sound like a fake founder.
- Do not make the founder account carry support obligations that should belong to product, docs, email, help, or company channels.
- Each channel should have its own CTA and metric, even when both point to the same waitlist or product surface.

- YouTube/Substack/newsletter should usually carry depth, category thinking, founder essays, product walkthroughs, and owned audience.
- The same idea can travel across channels, but not as identical copy pasted everywhere.

## Personal Account As Product Infrastructure

This is project-dependent.

If the founder's personal account is an acquisition, trust, research, category-education, problem-understanding, or launch channel, treat it as product infrastructure.

If the founder is not relevant to the buyer/user journey, mark it as optional or not applicable. Do not force founder-led communication into projects where it adds noise, creates brand risk, or distracts from product/channel reality.

```md
Personal account:
Founder-led relevance: core | supporting | optional | not-applicable | harmful
Role in product:
Audience:
Commercial ICP overlap:
What product surfaces it feeds:
Primary CTA path:
Voice/copy evidence source:
Visual evidence source:
Posts/comments/DMs collection method:
Consent/privacy boundaries:
What can become product truth:
What stays personal/context only:
Review cadence:
Owner:
```

Rules:

- Personal social content is not just marketing; it can feed ICP, positioning, onboarding, copy, feature priority, support, and proof.
- This only applies when the project profile marks founder-led relevance as `core` or `supporting`.
- Do not generate public founder content at scale until real founder posts, captions, screenshots, videos, interviews or approved writing have been analyzed.
- Treat captions, comments, DMs and replies as evidence only after categorizing them by user segment, problem, objection, language, CTA, and decision impacted.
- Do not expose private DMs, private stories, sensitive personal details, user/client data, or identifiable audience data without explicit approval.
- A personal account may be the top of the funnel, but the product must still become understandable without the founder personally explaining it every time.
- If founder-led relevance is `optional`, `not-applicable`, or `harmful`, do not let founder taste override ICP evidence, product language, UX clarity, pricing, compliance, or channel strategy.

```md

Limit:
Can publish now? yes/no

```

Block or require approval for:

- revenue, valuation, growth, virality, time saved, ROI, margin or market-size claims;
- user/client stories without consent;
- screenshots with private data;
- AI capability, data-source, automation, official integration or private-platform-data claims;
- competitor comparisons;
- fundraising, hiring, CTO, investor or legal-sensitive claims;
- personal/private founder stories not explicitly approved for public use.

Founder-led communication must feed the product.

```md

Hypothesis:
Audience:
CTA:
Metric:
Qualitative replies:
User interviews created:
Product/copy/support insight:
Decision changed:

```

Rules:

- Comments, DMs, replies, saves, shares, waitlist quality, and demo calls are product evidence only when categorized.
- Viral posts are not product evidence unless they bring qualified users or useful learning.
- Strong reactions should become research questions, not automatic roadmap changes.

## Feature / Launch Checklist

Before launch, waitlist, pricing, public demo, major copy change, or beta announcement:

```md
What will the founder say publicly?
Which channel(s)?
What user problem does it teach?
What proof can be shown?

```

## Audit Requirements

A full audit must produce:

- founder data package status;
- founder brand vs product brand map;
- audience vs ICP mismatch;
- content pillar matrix;
- channel role/cadence matrix;
- social/public claims risk table;
- proof asset inventory;
- launch narrative options;
- founder communication decision table;
- feedback-to-product loop;
- docs/biz/content reconciliation queue.

## Anti-Patterns

- Treating social as posting schedule instead of product-distribution system.
- Letting founder audience redefine ICP without evidence.
- Copying famous founders instead of adapting mechanisms.
- Overusing "authenticity" while sounding optimized or AI-written.
- Building a founder myth that the product cannot support.
- Publishing business claims before billing/data/product proof exists.
- Mixing personal account and product account without a bridge or boundary.
- Leaving founder communication in docs/biz only, disconnected from product, analytics, copy, launch, and support decisions.
